## Architecture and safeguards to preserve

- `Client` anchors documents, requirements, accounts, years/periods, journals, compliance and invoices. Requirement/document links are many-to-many; document and compliance follow-ups have separate subjects and must not be merged into one data model just because their interfaces resemble each other.
- Journals own debit/credit lines, retained evidence and an optional voucher. The General Ledger and financial reports derive from posted journals rather than duplicating transactions. Account templates initialize client-specific accounts; these are distinct configuration and operational records.
- Invoices own items and payments. Integer-cent totals and payment balances are derived. An invoice is not a journal voucher, and a recorded voucher does not prove bank clearing.
- Users have a role; roles have permission grants. `Access` limits Bookkeepers to assigned clients and authorized knowledge records. Policies, middleware and field-level `RecordInput` checks protect direct requests as well as visible controls. Hiding a button is never a permission change.
- Accounting uses same-client accounts/periods, exact positive balance checks, client/entry locks, independent review, review snapshots/digests, retained evidence, authorized posting and immutability safeguards. Preserve database protections and audit atomicity, not only model validations.
- Journal drafts can be unbalanced; voucher drafts have stricter requirements. Do not make their forms identical by weakening validation. Keep explicit period selection, account classification, debit/credit values and evidence-version changes understandable.
- Issued invoice editing, cancellation with payments, duplicate payment attempts and overpayments are restricted. “Mark paid” must never replace recording an actual payment.
- Document storage is private and downloads are authorized. Replacing reviewed/approved evidence requires explicit reopening and subsequent verification. Preserve history and attachment integrity.
- Authentication includes throttling and session-version invalidation. Super Admin is currently isolated from business data. Backups and restore verification exist as technical command/service workflows; no redesign should expose these as casual staff actions.

## Recommended navigation and task flows

Proposed primary destinations: **Overview, Clients, Documents, Accounting, Compliance, Billing, Knowledge, Reports**. Administration remains permission-specific pending the manuscript/RBAC decision. Avoid a separate top-level destination for every status or follow-up queue.

- Owner: overview of unresolved deadlines, review/posting work and balances; client and financial-report access; authorized business actions.
- Office Manager: document validation, onboarding readiness and compliance follow-ups; billing and knowledge maintenance; independent journal review/posting under current permissions.
- Bookkeeper: assigned client work, document intake, own journal drafts/corrections and financial reports. Billing/compliance editing must follow the explicit role decision below.
- Super Admin: current separate technical workspace until the role discrepancy is resolved; no automatic expansion of business access.

Proposed ordinary flow: select client once, work within that context, save, then see the next authorized action. Preserve global lists for cross-client work. Prefer one prominent action per state with secondary actions grouped by purpose. Destructive/financial state changes retain meaningful confirmation and validation.

Success criteria for later implementation: core modules directly reachable from navigation; no full sidebar duplication in tabs; local filters replace redundant destinations; client/date context persists; conditional sections reveal errors and existing data; keyboard and narrow-screen operation work; no unauthorized links or data; accounting results and invariants unchanged. Actual task-time improvements require timed user trials, not inference from a smaller menu.
